How it works

A thermostat, not a questionnaire

Other apps ask you twenty questions and hope. Dredfit asks one — after the workout — and knows.

1

Start easy — or calibrate

Day one is deliberately easy — every movement starts at the bottom of its range, and the regulator raises the load after each session until it settles at the edge of what you can actually do. Already training? Enter the number you actually did and the first workout calibrates the system: your level is set right away, no slow ramp-up.

2

One integer per movement

Your progress is one small number per movement — ten of them. Each level encodes the variation, the rep target and the set count at once: harder variations unlock automatically — starting their count lower, so the change lands softly — and above the top tier the sets grow instead.

tier = min(4, 1 + L / 8) · reps = start(tier) + L % 8
3

Answer one question

Tough, on plan, or easy — one tap and the next workout adjusts. Want to be exact? Enter the number you actually did: for that movement, a fact outweighs the rating. When a single movement falls short three times running, it alone rolls back a few steps and starts again. Progress without burnout.

What's inside

The workout

40 exercises, household propsClassic calisthenics progressions — knee push-ups to archer push-ups, squats to shrimp squats. A chair, a table, a wall, a towel and a step cover 36 of them; the four pull-up variations are the only ones that need a bar, and they stay off until you switch them on.
Balanced by designA pull in every session for shoulder health; the other patterns rotate so each comes round five times in eight workouts.
Got a pull-up bar?Turn it on and every other session swaps the floor pull for a vertical one — bar hang through full pull-ups, tracked separately.
Warm-up, cool-down, holds and rest, timedA guided warm-up and cool-down, a hold timer for planks, and a rest ring with a 3-2-1 countdown — stretch the rest as well as skip it. Five seconds to get ready before every position, and both blocks pause for the doorbell, resuming from the second they stopped. Side positions get a counted switch pause. Every timer is wall-clock based, so locking the phone loses nothing.
Plain-language techniqueThree steps and two common mistakes for every exercise, plus an “In life” line: what the movement gives you outside the app. Every warm-up and cool-down position gets its own short sheet with the steps. No jargon.

It adapts to you

Adjust in the momentDid fewer reps? Dial the real number right at the exercise, without leaving it. Skipping is free — a skipped exercise simply doesn't count.
Something hurt? That movement restsMuscles giving out and a joint hurting are different things, and the app answers them differently. Tap “Something hurt” during the workout and that movement rests: it keeps its place in the plan at the same level and stops getting harder for the next three times it comes round. One tap, one consequence — no questionnaire, no wellness score. And when nothing hurts but a movement needs longer at its level, “Hold this level” gives it the same pause — while the workout still counts.
Breaks are normalA week away, and every movement quietly eases one step. Two weeks or more, and the plan offers to meet you a few steps lower — further down the longer the break. Levels catch up quickly.
Short on time?Under Start there is a short version: three exercises of the six, warm-up and cool-down included — about 40% off the clock. The other three are recorded as honest skips, and the next day the full workout is the default again.
An interrupted session waits for youIf the phone closes the app mid-workout, the next launch offers to carry on where you stopped. Out of time? Finish early and the workout still counts — what you did is recorded, the rest is simply left undone.

Milestones, not medalsA workout that unlocks a harder variation, adds a set, or lands on a round number ends on one calm screen you can share. Anniversaries remember where you started: then → now. No confetti, no badges, no streaks to lose.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Dredfit really free?

Yes — no subscription, no in-app purchases, no ads, no locked features. The app you download is the whole app, and it's open source, so you can check.

Does it work offline?

Always. Dredfit makes no network requests — there are no servers. The plan, the timers and your whole history live on your phone.

Do I need equipment or a gym?

No. A chair, a table, a wall, a towel and a step cover 36 of the 40 exercises. The four pull-up variations are the only ones that need a bar, and they stay off until you switch them on.

Do I need an account? What data is collected?

There's nothing to sign up for and nothing is collected: no account, no analytics, no third-party SDKs. Your training history never leaves your phone unless you export it yourself.

What if I skip a week or two?

Nothing to apologise for. After a week away every movement quietly eases one step; after two weeks the plan offers to meet you a few steps lower — the longer the break, the lower — and levels catch up quickly.

I'm a complete beginner — will it fit?

That's the default. The first workout is deliberately easy — about 33 minutes, no equipment: it calibrates the system, and every next workout adjusts to how the last one actually went.

0

Zero data collected

No analytics, no ads, no third-party SDKs, no network requests at all. Dredfit has no servers and never transmits your training history — it is stored on your device and never reaches us. That's not a promise, it's the architecture.

It leaves your phone only when you decide: a JSON backup you export and share yourself, a milestone card you choose to share, workouts you opt into writing to Apple Health, or a standard iOS device backup. The privacy policy spells all four out.
